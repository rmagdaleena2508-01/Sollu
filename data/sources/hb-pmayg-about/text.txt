About Pradhan Mantri Awaas Yojana - Gramin (PMAY-G)
Pradhan Mantri Awaas Yojana - Gramin (PMAY-G) is the flagship rural housing programme implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development, Government of India, aimed at achieving the national commitment of providing "Housing for All" in rural India.
Programme Background & Evolution
1
Public housing programmes in India began with the rehabilitation of displaced families following independence. In January 1996, rural housing was established as an independent flagship initiative named Indira Awaas Yojana (IAY) .
In 2014, an audit by the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) identified key areas for improvement. These included assessing actual housing needs, ensuring open beneficiary selection, improving house quality, and combining benefits from other government welfare schemes.
2
To address these findings and deliver on the promise of "Housing for All", the scheme was restructured into Pradhan Mantri Awaas Yojana – Gramin (PMAY-G) on 1st April 2016, placing the citizen at the heart of programme delivery.
Objectives & Financial Assistance
3
PMAY-G provides financial grants directly to rural homeless families and those living in kutcha dwellings to build a solid, disaster-resilient pucca house with essential civic amenities.
Minimum House Size: 25 square metres, including a dedicated kitchen area.
Financial Grant (Plain Areas): ₹ 1,20,000 per house, delivered directly via bank transfer.
Financial Grant (Hilly & Difficult Areas): ₹ 1,30,000 per house in North Eastern, Himalayan States, and difficult districts.
Unskilled Labour Support: Up to 95 person-days of daily wage employment under MGNREGS (approx. ₹ 20,000+ additional benefit).
Sanitation Assistance: ₹ 12,000 for toilet construction in convergence with Swachh Bharat Mission-Gramin (SBM-G).
Essential Amenities: Piped water (Jal Jeevan Mission), electricity (Saubhagya), and clean cooking gas (Ujjwala Yojana) provided alongside house completion.
4
Cost Sharing Pattern: The construction cost is jointly funded by Central and State Governments. In plain areas, the ratio is 60:40 (Centre:State). In North-Eastern and Himalayan States, it is 90:10 . For Union Territories without legislature, 100% is funded by the Central Government.
Funds are credited directly to dedicated State bank accounts to ensure timely payment releases to registered beneficiaries at each construction milestone.
Transparent Beneficiary Selection & Appeals
5
How Beneficiaries Are Chosen: Rather than arbitrary selection, eligible families are identified based on housing deprivation and kutcha home criteria from the official SECC 2011 and updated AwaasPlus surveys.
Gram Sabha Scrutiny: Every draft list is read aloud and publicly validated at the local Gram Sabha village assembly. This ensures that no genuine family is left out and no ineligible family is included.
How to Raise a Complaint or Appeal: Any citizen whose name is wrongfully excluded or who notices ineligible entries can submit an appeal to the Block Development Officer (BDO) or District Appellate Committee, or file a grievance online via the national CPGRAMS Public Grievance Portal .
Core Values & Guiding Principles
Transparency
Public waitlists, objective SECC-based targeting, Gram Sabha verification, and publicly accessible real-time tracking prevent discretionary selection.
Equity & Social Justice
Unconditional priority to the most vulnerable: houseless households, SC/ST families, primitive tribal groups, and female-headed households.
Financial Accountability
Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) directly into beneficiary bank accounts via PFMS, with geo-tagged verification eliminating middlemen and leakage.
Construction Quality & Resilience
Disaster-resilient house typologies tailored to local climates, backed by technical oversight and nationwide rural mason certification.
Technical Oversight & e-Governance Architecture
6
National Technical Support Agency (NTSA): Provides technical oversight for construction standards. A pan-India Rural Mason Training and Certification programme ensures skilled masonry across all rural districts.
7
How Applications & Photos Are Tracked (AwaasSoft & AwaasApp): Every house is monitored through a digital workflow. Field functionaries use the AwaasApp mobile application to capture time-stamped, geo-tagged photographs at every construction stage (existing site, plinth, lintel, and completion).
Direct Bank Payments: Once each photo milestone is approved, the AwaasSoft portal automatically generates a Fund Transfer Order (FTO) through the Public Financial Management System (PFMS), depositing instalment money straight into the beneficiary's Aadhaar-linked bank account without middlemen.
8
Institutional Credit & Community Monitoring: Beneficiaries can avail optional low-interest bank loans up to ₹ 70,000 for house enlargement. Continuous oversight is conducted through public online dashboards, community Social Audits, District DISHA monitoring committees, and National Level Monitors (NLM).
